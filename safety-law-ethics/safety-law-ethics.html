Scenario

You install a free navigation app. It asks for location access "always," and its privacy policy says it may share "usage data" with partners. Before reading on, write down one thing you think the app could learn about you over a month.

Part 1: Ethics in Technology (5.3.4)

Ethics asks what should be done, even when nothing forces you to do it. In computing, ethical questions often arise where the law is silent, unclear, or has not caught up with new technology.

Key idea: location data is revealing

A single location point looks harmless. A month of location points can reveal where you sleep, where you work, where you worship, which clinic you visit, and who you spend time with. The ethical issue is not whether the app can collect this, but whether the people it collects from would reasonably expect and agree to it.

Think about it

  1. If a company promised to delete your location history after 30 days, would that fully solve the ethical concern? Why or why not?
  2. Is it ethical to collect data that users agreed to in a long policy few people read? What would make consent meaningful?
  3. Who benefits from location data, and who bears the risk?

Part 2: Laws and Regulations (5.3.5)

A law is a rule that is enforceable by a government. Ethics is a standard of right and wrong that may or may not be written into law. A practice can be legal but unethical, and an ethical concern may become illegal once a legislature acts on it.

Ethics or law?

For each example, decide whether the main issue is primarily ethical, legal, or both. Then read the explanation.

Three laws that touch location and personal data

LawJurisdictionWhat it does (summary)
General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Regulation (EU) 2016/679 European Union Treats location data as personal data. Requires a lawful basis (such as consent) before processing it, and gives people rights to access and delete their data.
California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the CPRA (California Civil Code §1798.100 et seq.) California, USA Gives California residents the right to know what personal information is collected and to opt out of its sale or sharing. Precise geolocation is treated as "sensitive personal information."
Federal Trade Commission Act, Section 5 United States (federal) Prohibits unfair or deceptive practices. The FTC has pursued companies whose privacy policies did not match what they actually did with data.

Note: Laws change. Verify current text on the official sites listed in the Sources section before relying on any summary here.
